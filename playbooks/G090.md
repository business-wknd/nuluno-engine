# Company sales: raise paid contracts from demos and pilots

- **Goal id:** `G090`
- **Channel:** partnerships
- **Metric:** `procurement_purchase_rate` = `paid_contracts` / `qualified_opportunities`, should increase
- **Observation window:** 168 hours after publishing
- **If the target is missed, the agent picks one bottleneck:** audience (the next round starts with `A-SEGMENTS`), motivation (the next round starts with `A-MOTIVES`), angle (the next round starts with `A-ANGLES`), fit (the next round starts with `C-PT-CLOSE`), proposal (the next round starts with `S27`), data (the next round starts with `C-PT-METRICS`)

## Steps

### 1. Read the product and what it sells

Build one dated fact sheet of what the product does, what it costs, and what is still unknown, so every later sentence of sales copy can be checked against something real.

- **S01.01** Open the product's own pages: home page, sales page, pricing page, and any help or delivery documents. Write down each URL and the date and time you read it.  
  _Done when:_ A list of URLs or file names, each with the time it was read.
- **S01.02** Look at the live product or a demo and list what a user can actually get done with it today. Ask the owner about anything you cannot see for yourself.  
  _Done when:_ Each listed function points to a screen, a document page, or the owner's confirmation.
- **S01.03** For each item on that list, mark whether the software does it, a person does it as a service, or it is not delivered yet.  
  _Done when:_ Every item is labeled software, human service, or not yet available, with who delivers it and any limit.
- **S01.04** Write down what is for sale: plan names, price, currency, billing period, current discounts, and when each discount ends. If a price is not shown on the product's pages, write 'unknown' and ask the owner.  
  _Done when:_ Every price and term has the URL or owner message it came from.
- **S01.05** Open the existing trial, sign-up, and buy links and check that each one loads and lands on the right product. Do not fill in forms and do not pay.  
  _Done when:_ Each link is listed with the page it actually reached and whether it worked.
- **S01.06** Collect proof that may be quoted: real customer cases, screenshots, numbers, and statements the owner has approved for public use.  
  _Done when:_ Each piece of proof has a source and a note on where it may be used.
- **S01.07** List what must never be promised without the owner's say: features that do not exist, new prices, extra deliverables, and business limits.  
  _Done when:_ A 'do not promise' list that later posts and replies can be checked against.
- **S01.08** Put everything into one product fact sheet: what is sold, proof, links, limits, and unknowns. Date it so later steps know which version they used.  
  _Done when:_ One dated fact sheet that the following steps can use without reopening the site.
- **S01.09** Note when the sources should be checked again, and update the fact sheet whenever the owner says something changed.  
  _Done when:_ A next-check date written on the fact sheet.

**Hand back:** `product_facts`, `offer_and_price`, `limits_and_unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `site_unreachable`: If the product pages will not load, ask the owner for a working URL or the page text, and do not describe the product from memory.
- `price_not_shown`: If no price is visible, record the price as unknown and ask the owner; never guess one.
- `product_behind_login`: If the product can only be seen after signing in, ask the owner for screenshots or a short walk-through; never ask for a password.
- `pages_disagree`: If two pages state different prices or features, list both versions with their URLs and ask the owner which one is current.
- `broken_buy_link`: If a sign-up or buy link fails or lands on the wrong page, report it to the owner before any post sends people there.

### 2. Collect real buyer quotes

Gather the exact words real people use for this problem and the result they want, because copy written in buyers' own words gets read and copy written in the seller's words gets skipped.

- **A-VOC.01** List where real buyers talk about this problem: interviews, reviews, public help threads, customer emails, and refund reasons. Mark which of these the owner allows you to use.  
  _Done when:_ A source list with the URL or origin of each source and whether its use is allowed.
- **A-VOC.02** Collect at least five posts or comments where real people describe this problem in their own words. For each one keep the shortest exact quote, its URL, its date, and an anonymous label such as 'person 1' instead of a name.  
  _Done when:_ At least five quotes, each with URL, date, and anonymous label.
- **A-VOC.03** Sort each quote under one of five headings: the situation they were in, what they do today, what it costs them, the result they want, and what makes them hesitate before buying.  
  _Done when:_ Every quote sits under one heading.
- **A-VOC.04** Remove sellers promoting themselves, AI-written answers, and guesses with no source. If you cannot read the original text, mark it as missing and do not paraphrase it from memory.  
  _Done when:_ A list of what was removed and why.
- **A-VOC.05** Note which kind of people the sample came from (people who have not bought, buyers, people who refunded) and state its limits. Do not present it as the whole market.  
  _Done when:_ A short note on who is in the sample and who is missing.

**Hand back:** `buyer_quotes`, `sources`, `patterns_found`, `limits_and_unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If you find fewer than five real quotes, ask the owner for customer emails or reviews, say which public places you searched, and label everything else as a guess.
- `source_unreadable`: If a page is blocked or needs sign-in, skip it or ask the owner for a copy; do not rebuild the quote from search snippets.
- `private_messages_without_permission`: If the only material is private customer messages, ask the owner whether they may be used and remove names before using them.
- `sensitive_personal_details`: If a quote exposes health, money, or relationship details about an identifiable person, drop the identifying details and keep only the need that relates to the product.

### 3. Group buyers by situation and trigger

Split buyers into groups by the moment the problem shows up, then choose one group to speak to, because copy aimed at one clear situation gets more response than copy aimed at everyone.

- **A-SEGMENTS.01** Read the collected quotes and the product fact sheet. Do not work out why people buy from their age or gender.  
  _Done when:_ Each observation points to a quote or a product fact.
- **A-SEGMENTS.02** Group buyers by five things: when the problem shows up, what they use instead today, what that costs them, the result they want, and the time, money, or skill they have.  
  _Done when:_ Two to four named groups, each described on those five points.
- **A-SEGMENTS.03** Attach at least one real quote to each group, or label the group as a guess. For each group, say who is not a fit.  
  _Done when:_ Each group shows its quote or the word 'guess', plus who it leaves out.
- **A-SEGMENTS.04** Leave ability to pay, who decides, budget, and timing as 'unknown' unless a source says otherwise. Do not answer these from a job title.  
  _Done when:_ A list of unknowns for each group.
- **A-SEGMENTS.05** Choose the one group you can reach on this goal's channel now and that the product can serve today. Record why you chose it and what would show the choice is wrong.  
  _Done when:_ The chosen group, the reason, and at least one case that argues against it.

**Hand back:** `buyer_groups`, `chosen_group`, `why_chosen`, `unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If there are no buyer quotes, go back and collect them; if the owner wants to continue anyway, label every group as a guess.
- `only_demographics`: If all you have is age, gender, or job title, do not build groups from it; ask the owner about three recent buyers and what happened just before they bought.
- `group_cannot_be_served`: If the best-supported group needs something the product does not do, choose the next group and report the gap to the owner.
- `groups_overlap`: If two groups differ only in name, keep the one with more quotes and drop the other.

### 4. Map each problem to a buying motive

Connect each stated problem to the deeper result the buyer wants, because people pay for a change in their life or work, not for a feature.

- **A-MOTIVES.01** For each group, reread the real quotes and separate the problem they have right now from the change they want in their life or work.  
  _Done when:_ Each group has a 'problem now' line and a 'change wanted' line, both tied to quotes.
- **A-MOTIVES.02** Give each motive a working label such as earning money or career, love or company, health, looks, being liked, saving time, independence, or belonging. Treat every label as a guess to be tested.  
  _Done when:_ A list of motives, each with its label and marked 'to be tested'.
- **A-MOTIVES.03** For each motive write the quote behind it, the situation, how confident you are, and any quote that points the other way. Do not infer sensitive weaknesses from personal data.  
  _Done when:_ Each motive has a quote, a confidence note (strong, some, or guess), and any opposing quote.
- **A-MOTIVES.04** Split each motive into three parts: the practical job they want done, how they want to feel, and how they want others to see them.  
  _Done when:_ Each motive lists its practical, emotional, and social part, with 'unknown' where there is no evidence.
- **A-MOTIVES.05** Keep only the parts the product can really help with. Do not inflate fear, and do not promise income, health, or relationship outcomes.  
  _Done when:_ A final list of motives the product can serve, plus the ones you dropped and why.

**Hand back:** `motives`, `supporting_quotes`, `guesses_to_test`, `limits`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If a motive has no quote behind it, mark it as a guess and go back to collect buyer quotes before relying on it.
- `motive_does_not_fit_a_label`: If a motive does not fit the common labels, describe it in the buyer's own words and do not force it into one.
- `product_cannot_help`: If the product does not really help with a motive, drop it from the selling plan and tell the owner what buyers wanted.
- `sensitive_guess`: If a motive depends on guessing someone's health, finances, or relationship status, remove it and use only needs people stated themselves.

### 5. Pick the strongest motive and objection

Choose the single motive and the single objection that matter most to the chosen group right now, so the post makes one strong point and not five weak ones.

- **A-PRIORITY.01** For the chosen group, read the list of motives, what they use today, and the event that makes them look for a solution. Count repeated quotes from the same person once.  
  _Done when:_ The group name and the quotes used, with repeats from one person merged.
- **A-PRIORITY.02** Compare the motives on four points: how strong the quotes are, how urgent the timing is, what the buyer already pays or loses, and how much of it the product can help with. Judge by this evidence, not by scoring a fixed list of desires.  
  _Done when:_ A short comparison showing each motive against the four points.
- **A-PRIORITY.03** Name the main motive, the second motive, and the cases that contradict them. If the evidence is thin, keep two candidates and say so.  
  _Done when:_ Main and second motive, opposing cases, and a plain statement of how sure you are.
- **A-PRIORITY.04** Separate people who have the budget and the authority to buy from people who only want it. List unknown buying obstacles as questions to ask.  
  _Done when:_ Known objections, plus a list of open questions for the owner or for buyers.
- **A-PRIORITY.05** Pass the main motive on to the benefits step and the angle test. Write down beforehand which real replies or payments would prove it wrong.  
  _Done when:_ The main motive in one sentence and what result would disprove it.

**Hand back:** `main_motive`, `main_objection`, `second_motive`, `what_would_disprove_it`, `open_questions`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If the ranking rests on no buyer quotes, say it is a guess and go back to collect quotes before treating it as settled.
- `evidence_too_thin`: If the quotes cannot separate two motives, keep both and let the angle test compare them.
- `cannot_tell_who_can_pay`: If nothing shows who has the budget or the say, leave it unknown and ask the owner who has actually paid before.
- `owner_disagrees`: If the owner believes a different motive is strongest, record both views with their evidence and test both.

### 6. Turn features into benefits you can prove

Translate each real feature into the benefit and result a buyer cares about, and mark exactly where proof stops, so the post is persuasive and still true.

- **A-LADDER.01** Read the product facts, the limits, and the buyer quotes one at a time. Do not add anything the product cannot do.  
  _Done when:_ The facts and quotes you used, each traceable to the fact sheet or a source URL.
- **A-LADDER.02** For each feature write a chain of four items: the feature, the benefit a buyer can observe, the result they want, and the point past which nothing may be promised.  
  _Done when:_ One chain per feature with all four items filled in.
- **A-LADDER.03** Mark each link in every chain as verified, guess, or unsupported, and give the reason.  
  _Done when:_ Every link carries one of the three marks and its basis.
- **A-LADDER.04** Check every number and every cause-and-effect claim separately. For example, 'meet people from five industries' and 'raise your chance of a new job' are two claims, and each needs its own proof.  
  _Done when:_ A list of claims, each with its proof or the note 'no proof, do not state as fact'.
- **A-LADDER.05** Choose the value statement that is both true and matches the main motive, and write it as one to three short message options ready to test.  
  _Done when:_ The chosen statement and its message options, each tied to verified facts.

**Hand back:** `chosen_message`, `message_options`, `facts_used`, `must_not_promise`

**If stuck:**
- `no_product_facts`: If the product fact sheet is missing or out of date, go back and read the product before writing any benefit.
- `claim_has_no_proof`: If a benefit has no proof, word it as an invitation to try or as something being tested, never as a result.
- `number_without_source`: If a number has no source, remove it or ask the owner where it comes from.
- `benefit_needs_missing_feature`: If the benefit buyers want depends on a feature that does not exist, drop it from the copy and tell the owner about the gap.

### 7. Design the sales angle test

Write two or three ways to present the same true benefit, choose the one this round publishes, and write the win rule before posting, so the goal's own number, compared with the baseline, decides whether it works. The other angles are kept for later rounds.

- **A-ANGLES.01** Name one buyer group in one situation, the quote that represents them, the motive, and the benefit the product can deliver.  
  _Done when:_ Group, quote, motive, and benefit written in four short lines.
- **A-ANGLES.02** Write two or three angles: the progress they hope for, getting past the real obstacle, and a believable demonstration. Do not use fear to exaggerate outcomes.  
  _Done when:_ Two or three angles, each in one or two sentences.
- **A-ANGLES.03** For each angle list the facts that support it, the promises that are still unknown, and the wording that must not be used.  
  _Done when:_ Each angle has supporting facts, unknowns, and banned wording next to it.
- **A-ANGLES.04** Choose the one angle this round publishes. The round compares it with the baseline, not with the other angles; keep those unchanged for later rounds. Keep the same channel, audience, and destination link as the baseline. If a channel step later offers a second version (?v=B), it differs in one labeled thing only and is extra information; the verdict still comes from the round's total. Before posting, write the guess being tested, how long the round runs, the number that counts as a win, and the cost in time or money.  
  _Done when:_ A test plan written before posting, showing the chosen angle, the guess, the duration, the win number, the cost, and the angles kept for later rounds.
- **A-ANGLES.05** Choose the main number: the goal's metric, shown with this step (for example link clicks divided by views, followers gained, or purchases). Likes are supporting information only. State when the number is read.  
  _Done when:_ The main number, when it is read, and the chosen angle ready to be written as the post.

**Hand back:** `test_guess`, `angles`, `facts_used`, `win_rule`

**If stuck:**
- `no_main_motive`: If no main motive has been chosen, go back and choose one before writing angles.
- `angle_needs_unproven_claim`: If an angle only works with a claim that has no proof, rewrite it as an invitation to try or drop the angle.
- `too_few_views_expected`: If the post is unlikely to get enough views to compare with the baseline, say so and report the result as an early signal, not as proof.
- `cannot_measure_main_number`: If the goal's main number cannot be read anywhere, ask the owner how it can be checked, and do not declare a winner from likes.

### 8. Confirm the sales accounts, offer and partner terms

Settle which account contacts partners and buyers, which offer and terms can be used today, and how referrals are counted, so every later message goes out from the right place and every sale can be counted.

- **C-PT-ACCESS.01** Ask the owner which account will send messages to partners and buyers, for example the business email address or a partner program dashboard. Ask where past conversations are kept: the inbox, a spreadsheet, or, if the owner already uses a CRM, that CRM. Write down where you will read conversations and, separately, which account will send.  
  _Done when:_ The sending account, where past conversations are kept, and who can open each.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.02** Have the owner sign in to the sending account themselves, or confirm the agent's browser is already signed in to it. Check that the name and address shown match the brand. Never ask for a password. If the owner asked for a new sign-up, for example on an affiliate platform they chose, use inbox_address for the email and inbox_latest for the verification code, create only one real account, and follow that platform's rules.  
  _Done when:_ The signed-in account name and address, who signed in, and any new account the owner asked for.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.03** Use read_page on the product's pricing page and write down the offer that can be sold today: plans, prices, currency, trial length, refund policy, and any discount that already exists. Have the owner confirm it. Do not create new plans, discounts, bundles or billing.  
  _Done when:_ An offer sheet with each line linked to the page it came from, and the owner's confirmation.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.04** Ask the owner for existing partner terms: the commission or referral reward, when it is paid, how long a referral counts, and which logos, screenshots and claims partners may use. If no terms exist, write 'no terms yet' and list two or three options for the owner to decide. Never promise a commission yourself.  
  _Done when:_ The existing terms quoted from their source, or 'no terms yet' with the options shown to the owner.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.05** Check how referrals will be counted. If the owner already runs an affiliate or referral program, record its link format. If not, do not set up a new program; call tracking for this round's tracked link and plan one label per partner (?v=A for the first, ?v=B for the second, and so on). Confirm with the owner that the sale counter from tracking is on the page shown after payment.  
  _Done when:_ The referral link format, or the round's tracked link with its labels, and whether the sale counter is in place.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.06** List who this round may contact and why: partners who publicly invite partnership requests, and buyers who asked a question, started a trial, or bought before. Get the list of people who unsubscribed or said no, and remove them. Having worked with someone before is not permission to send them bulk messages.  
  _Done when:_ The contact list with the reason each person may be contacted, and the opt-out list that was applied.
- **C-PT-ACCESS.07** Agree with the owner on limits for this round, for example at most 10 new partner contacts and at most 2 follow-ups per person, and on who presses send. Write the summary in five lines and get the owner's yes.  
  _Done when:_ The five-line summary and the owner's approval message.

**Hand back:** `sending_account`, `current_offer`, `partner_terms`, `referral_tracking`, `contact_rules`

**If stuck:**
- `wrong_account_signed_in`: If the signed-in account is personal or belongs to another brand, stop and ask the owner to switch accounts or to send the messages themselves.
- `owner_action_needed`: If a verification step, terms screen or identity check appears that inbox_latest cannot answer, stop and tell the owner which screen and which button.
- `terms_not_existing`: If there are no partner terms, give the owner options to choose from; do not sign anything or promise a commission.
- `no_sale_counter`: If the sale counter is not on the after-payment page, ask the owner to add it before any outreach, or to name the payment report that will be used instead.
- `offer_unclear`: If the price or plans on the page do not match what the owner says, use neither until the owner confirms which one is correct.
- `no_opt_out_list`: If nobody can say who unsubscribed or said no, contact only people who wrote to the owner in the last 90 days and did not ask to stop.

### 9. Move each open buyer to their next real step

Sort every open inquiry, trial, stalled deal, past customer and company lead by where they really are, and move each forward by one buyer action, because only confirmed buyer actions turn into paid sales.

- **C-PT-CLOSE.01** Collect every open buyer in the inbox and, if the owner already uses a CRM, that CRM: people who asked a question, started a trial, went quiet, or are a company lead in the last 90 days, plus customers who bought in the last 12 months. Write one row each with the last message date and who wrote last.  
  _Done when:_ A buyer list with one row per person or company, the last message date, and who wrote last.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.02** Give each row one stage: new inquiry, active trial, stalled (no reply for 21 days or more), past customer, or company deal. Remove anyone who said no or unsubscribed.  
  _Done when:_ Each row with its stage, and the removed people with the reason.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.03** For each buyer, copy from their own messages what they need, their problem, budget, timing, and who decides. Mark a field 'not asked yet' when they have not said it; never fill it with a guess.  
  _Done when:_ Each buyer's needs in their own words, with missing fields marked.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.04** Compare each need with what the product does today, using the product fact sheet. If the product cannot do it, mark 'no fit', write the reason, and note it as a product request for the owner. Do not promise future features.  
  _Done when:_ Each buyer marked fit or no fit, with the reason and the fact it rests on.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.05** Choose one next buyer action for each fit: answer their question, a short follow-up, a tailored demo, a business case, a small pilot, or help with company purchasing. Pick the one that removes what is stopping them now.  
  _Done when:_ One next action per buyer and the obstacle it removes.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.06** Draft any direct replies needed, answering the buyer's actual question with existing offers only and the round's tracked link when pointing to the product. Show the owner each reply word for word and get a yes. The agent sends only if it has the tools and the owner said yes; otherwise the owner sends.  
  _Done when:_ Each approved reply, its recipient, the send time, and who sent it.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.07** Count a milestone only when the buyer acts: books a call, starts a trial, asks for a quote, or pays. 'Sounds interesting' or a proposal you sent is not a milestone.  
  _Done when:_ Milestones per buyer, each with the message or record that shows the buyer's action.
- **C-PT-CLOSE.08** Update the buyer list with each next step, its date, and who does it. Pass each buyer to the matching step: the follow-up step, the demo step, the business case step, the pilot step, or the company purchase step.  
  _Done when:_ The updated list with next step, date and person for every open buyer.

**Hand back:** `buyer_stages`, `next_actions`, `replies_sent`, `milestones`

**If stuck:**
- `buyer_no_fit`: If the product cannot do what the buyer needs, close the lead with the reason and do not push.
- `no_open_buyers`: If there are no open buyers or recent customers, tell the owner and go back to finding partners or buyers.
- `owner_has_not_approved`: If the owner has not said yes to a reply, do not send it; show them the text and wait.
- `buyer_asks_for_new_terms`: If a buyer asks for a discount or terms that do not exist, pass the question to the owner and promise nothing.
- `decision_maker_unknown`: If you do not know who decides, ask the buyer that one question in the next approved reply.
- `opted_out`: If a buyer has unsubscribed or said no, remove them and never contact them again.

### 10. Read this goal's partner and sales numbers (baseline numbers)

Record the two numbers this goal is judged on for a fixed window, counted once per real buyer and net of refunds and tests, so rounds are compared on real sales rather than interest.

- **C-PT-METRICS.01** Call where_are_we and copy this goal's exact metric component names and its observation window. Each goal has its own pair, for example qualified_referrals and referred_people. Use only the names this goal lists, and never borrow numbers from another goal.  
  _Done when:_ The goal id, its component names copied exactly, and its observation window in hours.
- **C-PT-METRICS.02** Fix the window and time zone. For the baseline, use the window just before this round started; for the current reading, use the same length of time since the round started (168 hours for these goals). Do not take the current reading before the window ends.  
  _Done when:_ Start and end of the window with time zone, and whether it is the baseline or the current reading.
- **C-PT-METRICS.03** Gather the sources: clicks and sales from tracking, the payment system's report, the inbox threads, the partner program's own report if one exists, and, if the owner already uses a CRM, that CRM. If you cannot open a source, ask the owner for a screenshot or export of that exact window.  
  _Done when:_ Each source named, with the time you read it or the file the owner sent.
- **C-PT-METRICS.04** Build one list with one row per person or company, merging duplicates by email or company name; a sale that shows in both the payment report and a partner report counts once. Before counting, write the rule for each component, for example 'qualified means matches the buyer group and had a real two-way conversation or started a trial', or 'dormant means no reply for 21 days or more'.  
  _Done when:_ The deduplicated list and the written counting rule for each component.
- **C-PT-METRICS.05** Remove anything that is not a real outside buyer: the owner's and team's own purchases, test payments, bots, the agent's own messages, refunded orders and duplicates. Credit a sale to a partner or follow-up only if a partner link, a label, or the buyer's own words show it; otherwise mark its source unknown.  
  _Done when:_ The count removed for each reason, and how many sales have an unknown source.
- **C-PT-METRICS.06** Count money as received, not promised: a signed contract or an unpaid invoice is not a purchase. Note refunds, delivery costs, and commissions owed in the window; an unpaid commission is owed, not paid.  
  _Done when:_ Paid, unpaid and refunded amounts listed separately, with commissions owed.
- **C-PT-METRICS.07** Call record_numbers with each component under its exact name and a source that says where each number was read. If there is no history, record 0 and say 'no history' in source. Never estimate. If the denominator is 0, say so in source and do not report a rate change.  
  _Done when:_ The values and sources passed to record_numbers, as accepted.
- **C-PT-METRICS.08** Write one line on where the numbers stall: too few people entering, people not qualifying, or qualified people not acting or paying. This points the next round at the partner, the message, or the follow-up.  
  _Done when:_ One line naming the stalled stage and the numbers behind it.

**If stuck:**
- `no_history`: If there is no history for these numbers, record 0 and say 'no history' in source; the first round then sets the baseline.
- `cannot_open_payments`: If you cannot open the payment report, ask the owner for an export or screenshot of the exact window; never estimate sales.
- `attribution_unknown`: If a sale cannot be tied to a partner or follow-up, count its source as unknown and do not credit the partner.
- `numbers_include_tests`: If the owner's, team's or test payments are mixed in and cannot be separated, ask the owner to mark them before you record anything.
- `window_not_finished`: If the window has not ended, report the reading as early and read again when it ends.
- `denominator_zero`: If the denominator is 0, record 0, say so in source, and do not claim any rate.

### 11. Show the product working on the buyer's own task

Prepare and run a short demo of what the product already does, built around the one task the buyer cares about most, because seeing their own case handled is what moves a trial or a company lead to buy.

- **S26.01** Read the buyer's messages or call notes and pick the one task they care about most and how they will judge success. If the need is not clear, go back to the sales follow-through step to ask.  
  _Done when:_ The buyer's main task and how they judge success, quoted from their own words.
- **S26.02** List each part of that task and check that the product does it today. Mark any part it cannot do as a limit; do not promise future work.  
  _Done when:_ A list of task parts, each marked 'works today' or 'limit'.
- **S26.03** Choose the demo form: a live screen share by the owner, an existing recorded video, screenshots, or a demo account the owner already has. Open it and confirm it works. Never present mock-ups or edited images as the real product.  
  _Done when:_ The demo form chosen and its link or file, opened and confirmed to work.
- **S26.04** Prepare the sample data and the order of the demo. Use only data the buyer gave for this purpose or sample data clearly labeled as made up; never show another customer's private data, and never change the buyer's live data.  
  _Done when:_ The demo order in steps and the data used, with its source.
- **S26.05** For each part of the demo, write the one thing it proves for the buyer, plus answers to the two or three questions they are most likely to ask. Drop any claim the demo cannot show. Keep the whole demo under 15 minutes.  
  _Done when:_ A demo script linking each part to what it proves, plus the likely questions and answers.
- **S26.06** Rehearse once: open every screen, link and video in demo order. Fix or replace anything that fails.  
  _Done when:_ A rehearsal note listing what was checked and anything fixed.
- **S26.07** Write the invitation or the message with the recorded demo, using the round's tracked link if it points to the product. Show the owner the message word for word and send only after their yes; the agent sends only if it has the tools, otherwise the owner sends. Count the demo as given only if it took place or the buyer replied about it.  
  _Done when:_ The approved message, its send time, and whether the demo took place or the buyer replied about it.
- **S26.08** Record the buyer's actual reactions and questions in their own words. Silence is not agreement.  
  _Done when:_ The buyer's reactions and questions, quoted, with the date.
- **S26.09** Choose the next step from the result: a business case, a pilot, a follow-up, or closing the lead with the reason.  
  _Done when:_ The next step chosen and the buyer reaction it rests on.

**Hand back:** `demo_link`, `demo_script`, `buyer_feedback`, `limits`

**If stuck:**
- `need_unclear`: If you do not know the buyer's main task, go back to the sales follow-through step and ask them before building a demo.
- `product_cannot_do_it`: If the product cannot do the buyer's main task, tell the owner and mark the lead as no fit; do not promise it.
- `no_working_demo`: If no demo form works, ask the owner to run a live screen share; never show mock-ups as the product.
- `private_data_needed`: If the demo would need another customer's data or the buyer's live data, use labeled sample data instead or ask the data owner.
- `owner_has_not_approved`: If the owner has not said yes to the invitation, do not send it; show them the text and wait.
- `buyer_did_not_attend`: If the buyer missed the demo, offer one new time in an approved message and do not mark the demo as given.

### 12. Build a business case the buyer can forward

Turn the buyer's own figures and the current price into a clear cost and benefit calculation and a one-page proposal, because company buyers need a reason they can show their manager and finance before they pay.

- **S27.01** Find out who will read the proposal and what each person must decide: the user, their manager, finance, purchasing. If the buyer's need is not clear, return to the sales follow-through step first.  
  _Done when:_ Each reader and the decision they make.
- **S27.02** Write down the plan and price that fit, as shown on the product page or in existing terms: price, currency, billing period, what is delivered, and any trial. Do not invent discounts or bundles.  
  _Done when:_ The plan, price and terms, with their source.
- **S27.03** Ask the buyer for their own starting figures: hours spent on the task, people involved, current tool costs, and their target. Mark each number as 'from buyer' or 'assumption'; leave missing numbers blank, never zero.  
  _Done when:_ A table of inputs, each marked 'from buyer' or 'assumption', with blanks noted.
- **S27.04** Set up the calculation: the same period and currency for every figure, the unit, and the comparison (today versus with the product). Time saved is not new revenue unless the buyer says how it turns into money.  
  _Done when:_ The formula written in plain words, so anyone can redo it.
- **S27.05** Work out a low case and an expected case. Keep facts and estimates apart, and describe in words anything you cannot estimate in numbers.  
  _Done when:_ Low and expected results, with facts and estimates labeled.
- **S27.06** Check that no benefit, cost or commission is counted twice and that the totals add up. Fix any mismatch before writing.  
  _Done when:_ A check note confirming the sums and no double counting.
- **S27.07** Write a one-page proposal: the buyer's problem in their words, what the product does about it, the calculation, the price, the next step (an existing trial, a pilot or an order) with what their purchasing will need, and the limits. No made-up certifications, client names or guaranteed returns.  
  _Done when:_ The proposal text and the source for each claim.
- **S27.08** Show the owner the proposal and its cover message word for word and get a yes. Send one version from the agreed account; the agent sends only if it has the tools and the owner said yes, otherwise the owner sends.  
  _Done when:_ The approved version, its recipient, and the send time.
- **S27.09** Record the buyer's reply. Answer their questions, update the calculation only with numbers the buyer confirms, and move to the pilot step or the company purchase step when they ask for it.  
  _Done when:_ The buyer's reply quoted, and the version number of any update.

**Hand back:** `calculation`, `inputs_and_assumptions`, `proposal_text`, `buyer_reply`

**If stuck:**
- `need_unclear`: If the buyer's need or decision makers are unclear, go back to the sales follow-through step before writing any figures.
- `buyer_figures_missing`: If the buyer has not given their figures, ask for them; show ranges with clearly labeled assumptions and never fill gaps with zero.
- `new_terms_requested`: If the buyer wants a price or terms that do not exist, pass the request to the owner and do not put it in the proposal.
- `numbers_do_not_add_up`: If the totals do not match, fix the calculation before sending; never cover a gap with sales wording.
- `owner_has_not_approved`: If the owner has not said yes to the exact proposal, do not send it; show them the text and wait.

### 13. Run a small pilot with success the buyer agreed

Run a short, clearly bounded trial of the existing product against success measures the buyer agreed to, because real results on the buyer's own work are what justify a paid contract.

- **S28.01** Decide whether a pilot helps: it must answer one specific doubt the buyer has, and the product must already do the task. If it needs new features, do not run a pilot this round.  
  _Done when:_ The buyer's doubt the pilot answers, and confirmation the product does the task today.
- **S28.02** Write the pilot terms from existing offers only: what is included, how it is used, the price or the existing free trial it uses, its length (for example 14 days), and who delivers. Never invent free services or extra work.  
  _Done when:_ The pilot terms with their source.
- **S28.03** Agree the success measures with the buyer: one to three measures, where the data comes from, and the decision date. Until the buyer agrees in writing, the pilot has not started.  
  _Done when:_ The measures and the buyer's written agreement.
- **S28.04** List milestones: who uses the product, what result they report, and by when. Check each one can be done with the product as it is.  
  _Done when:_ A dated milestone list with a named person for each.
- **S28.05** Write the message with the pilot plan. Show the owner the message word for word and send only after their yes; the agent sends only if it has the tools, otherwise the owner sends. Confirm the start date and participants with the buyer.  
  _Done when:_ The approved message, its send time, and the buyer's confirmation of start date and participants.
- **S28.06** Track progress at each milestone from what the buyer or the delivering person reports. The buyer uses the product; the agent never uses it on the buyer's behalf to make results look better.  
  _Done when:_ A progress note per milestone with the source of each update.
- **S28.07** When something stalls, sort it as a usage, data or product problem. Fix usage and data problems with an explanation or a short demo; for a product problem, note it and stop promising that result.  
  _Done when:_ Each problem, its type, and what was done about it.
- **S28.08** At the end, mark each success measure met, not met, or unknown, with the evidence. Do not drop failed items.  
  _Done when:_ A results table with evidence for each measure.
- **S28.09** Recommend the next step from the results: the business case step or the company purchase step if the measures were met, or closing the pilot with the reason. Never call an unmet pilot a success.  
  _Done when:_ The recommendation and the results it rests on.
- **S28.10** Close the pilot: note payment received, amounts still unpaid, and delivery costs separately, and stop reminders that are no longer needed.  
  _Done when:_ Paid, unpaid and cost figures, and the pilot marked closed.

**Hand back:** `pilot_plan`, `success_measures`, `results`, `recommendation`

**If stuck:**
- `pilot_needs_new_features`: If the pilot would need features the product does not have, do not run it this round and tell the owner why.
- `buyer_has_not_agreed`: If the buyer has not agreed the success measures in writing, keep the pilot as not started and ask them to confirm.
- `owner_has_not_approved`: If the owner has not said yes to the pilot plan message, do not send it; show them the text and wait.
- `buyer_not_using`: If the buyer has not used the product by the first milestone, send one approved check-in; if there is still no use, close the pilot as not met.
- `data_not_available`: If the data for a measure cannot be obtained, agree a measurable replacement with the buyer before the end date.
- `new_terms_requested`: If the buyer asks for free extras or new terms, pass the request to the owner and promise nothing.

### 14. Guide a company purchase through to payment

Track who must approve, which documents are needed, and what the owner must decide, all the way to money received, because a company deal only counts when it is paid.

- **S29.01** From the buyer's messages and the proposal, write down where the purchase stands and who still has to approve: user, manager, finance, purchasing, legal. A user liking the product is not company approval.  
  _Done when:_ The approval chain with each person's role and status.
- **S29.02** Ask the buyer for their actual process: required documents, forms, deadlines, and the order of steps. List anything not yet known as a question to ask; do not assume another company's process.  
  _Done when:_ The buyer's purchase steps and deadlines, with open questions listed.
- **S29.03** Collect real documents the owner already has, such as company details, tax forms, an invoice template, the product description, and security or privacy answers. Never create or claim a certification the owner does not hold.  
  _Done when:_ Each document, its source, and whether it is current.
- **S29.04** Sort each buyer question: answered by a document, answered by existing terms, or needing the owner's decision. Give each one a person and a next step.  
  _Done when:_ The question list with type, person and next step for each.
- **S29.05** For negotiation, lay out options only within limits the owner has given in writing, with the effect of each on price, delivery and dates. Anything outside those limits goes to the owner; never offer a discount yourself.  
  _Done when:_ The options, each tied to an owner-set limit, and the items waiting for the owner.
- **S29.06** Prepare the documents and answers for the right person at the buyer. Show the owner the message and attachments word for word and send only after their yes; the agent sends only if it has the tools, otherwise the owner sends. A draft the buyer has not accepted is still a draft.  
  _Done when:_ The approved message, attachments, recipient, and send time.
- **S29.07** Follow up on approvals at the pace the buyer set, at most once a week unless they ask for more. When the buyer delays, update the expected date; never announce the deal as closed.  
  _Done when:_ Each follow-up date and the buyer's latest status.
- **S29.08** Hand anything that signs a contract, accepts new terms, or creates a duty to pay to the owner, with the exact text to decide on. The agent never signs or accepts terms.  
  _Done when:_ The items sent to the owner, the exact text, and the owner's decision or 'waiting'.
- **S29.09** Check the order, contract, invoice and payment separately. Count the deal as paid only when the money has arrived, net of refunds.  
  _Done when:_ Signed, invoiced and paid status with amounts and dates.
- **S29.10** Close the deal record. After payment, pass the customer on for delivery and later repeat-purchase follow-up; if the buyer walks away, record why and stop following up.  
  _Done when:_ The closed record with the outcome and the reason.

**Hand back:** `approval_chain`, `documents_sent`, `owner_decisions`, `paid_vs_unpaid`

**If stuck:**
- `approver_unknown`: If you do not know who approves the purchase, ask the buyer that one question in the next approved message.
- `document_not_held`: If the buyer asks for a document or certification the owner does not have, tell the owner and the buyer plainly; never create one.
- `terms_need_owner`: If a step signs a contract, accepts new terms or changes the price, stop and send the exact text to the owner to decide.
- `owner_has_not_approved`: If the owner has not said yes to a message or attachment, do not send it; show them and wait.
- `buyer_delayed`: If the buyer misses their own deadline, update the expected date and follow up once a week at most; do not report the deal as won.
- `contract_not_paid`: If a signed contract is not yet paid, record it as unpaid and do not count it as a paid contract.

### 15. Read this goal's partner and sales numbers (result numbers)

Record the two numbers this goal is judged on for a fixed window, counted once per real buyer and net of refunds and tests, so rounds are compared on real sales rather than interest.

- **C-PT-METRICS.01** Call where_are_we and copy this goal's exact metric component names and its observation window. Each goal has its own pair, for example qualified_referrals and referred_people. Use only the names this goal lists, and never borrow numbers from another goal.  
  _Done when:_ The goal id, its component names copied exactly, and its observation window in hours.
- **C-PT-METRICS.02** Fix the window and time zone. For the baseline, use the window just before this round started; for the current reading, use the same length of time since the round started (168 hours for these goals). Do not take the current reading before the window ends.  
  _Done when:_ Start and end of the window with time zone, and whether it is the baseline or the current reading.
- **C-PT-METRICS.03** Gather the sources: clicks and sales from tracking, the payment system's report, the inbox threads, the partner program's own report if one exists, and, if the owner already uses a CRM, that CRM. If you cannot open a source, ask the owner for a screenshot or export of that exact window.  
  _Done when:_ Each source named, with the time you read it or the file the owner sent.
- **C-PT-METRICS.04** Build one list with one row per person or company, merging duplicates by email or company name; a sale that shows in both the payment report and a partner report counts once. Before counting, write the rule for each component, for example 'qualified means matches the buyer group and had a real two-way conversation or started a trial', or 'dormant means no reply for 21 days or more'.  
  _Done when:_ The deduplicated list and the written counting rule for each component.
- **C-PT-METRICS.05** Remove anything that is not a real outside buyer: the owner's and team's own purchases, test payments, bots, the agent's own messages, refunded orders and duplicates. Credit a sale to a partner or follow-up only if a partner link, a label, or the buyer's own words show it; otherwise mark its source unknown.  
  _Done when:_ The count removed for each reason, and how many sales have an unknown source.
- **C-PT-METRICS.06** Count money as received, not promised: a signed contract or an unpaid invoice is not a purchase. Note refunds, delivery costs, and commissions owed in the window; an unpaid commission is owed, not paid.  
  _Done when:_ Paid, unpaid and refunded amounts listed separately, with commissions owed.
- **C-PT-METRICS.07** Call record_numbers with each component under its exact name and a source that says where each number was read. If there is no history, record 0 and say 'no history' in source. Never estimate. If the denominator is 0, say so in source and do not report a rate change.  
  _Done when:_ The values and sources passed to record_numbers, as accepted.
- **C-PT-METRICS.08** Write one line on where the numbers stall: too few people entering, people not qualifying, or qualified people not acting or paying. This points the next round at the partner, the message, or the follow-up.  
  _Done when:_ One line naming the stalled stage and the numbers behind it.

**If stuck:**
- `no_history`: If there is no history for these numbers, record 0 and say 'no history' in source; the first round then sets the baseline.
- `cannot_open_payments`: If you cannot open the payment report, ask the owner for an export or screenshot of the exact window; never estimate sales.
- `attribution_unknown`: If a sale cannot be tied to a partner or follow-up, count its source as unknown and do not credit the partner.
- `numbers_include_tests`: If the owner's, team's or test payments are mixed in and cannot be separated, ask the owner to mark them before you record anything.
- `window_not_finished`: If the window has not ended, report the reading as early and read again when it ends.
- `denominator_zero`: If the denominator is 0, record 0, say so in source, and do not claim any rate.
