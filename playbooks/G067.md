# Search: raise the share of qualified visits from outside articles

- **Goal id:** `G067`
- **Language:** English
- **Channel:** search
- **Metric:** `qualified_session_rate` = `qualified_sessions` / `attributed_sessions`, should increase
- **Observation window:** 168 hours after publishing
- **If the target is missed, the agent picks one bottleneck:** audience (the next round starts with `A-SEGMENTS`), motivation (the next round starts with `A-MOTIVES`), angle (the next round starts with `A-ANGLES`), query (the next round starts with `C-SE-QUERY`), message (the next round starts with `A-ANGLES`), data (the next round starts with `C-SE-METRICS`)

## Steps

### 1. Read the product and what it sells

Build one dated fact sheet of what the product does, what it costs, and what is still unknown, so every later sentence of sales copy can be checked against something real.

- **S01.01** Open the product's own pages: home page, sales page, pricing page, and any help or delivery documents. Write down each URL and the date and time you read it.  
  _Done when:_ A list of URLs or file names, each with the time it was read.
- **S01.02** Look at the live product or a demo and list what a user can actually get done with it today. Ask the owner about anything you cannot see for yourself.  
  _Done when:_ Each listed function points to a screen, a document page, or the owner's confirmation.
- **S01.03** For each item on that list, mark whether the software does it, a person does it as a service, or it is not delivered yet.  
  _Done when:_ Every item is labeled software, human service, or not yet available, with who delivers it and any limit.
- **S01.04** Write down what is for sale: plan names, price, currency, billing period, current discounts, and when each discount ends. If a price is not shown on the product's pages, write 'unknown' and ask the owner.  
  _Done when:_ Every price and term has the URL or owner message it came from.
- **S01.05** Open the existing trial, sign-up, and buy links and check that each one loads and lands on the right product. Do not fill in forms and do not pay.  
  _Done when:_ Each link is listed with the page it actually reached and whether it worked.
- **S01.06** Collect proof that may be quoted: real customer cases, screenshots, numbers, and statements the owner has approved for public use.  
  _Done when:_ Each piece of proof has a source and a note on where it may be used.
- **S01.07** List what must never be promised without the owner's say: features that do not exist, new prices, extra deliverables, and business limits.  
  _Done when:_ A 'do not promise' list that later posts and replies can be checked against.
- **S01.08** Put everything into one product fact sheet: what is sold, proof, links, limits, and unknowns. Date it so later steps know which version they used.  
  _Done when:_ One dated fact sheet that the following steps can use without reopening the site.
- **S01.09** Note when the sources should be checked again, and update the fact sheet whenever the owner says something changed.  
  _Done when:_ A next-check date written on the fact sheet.

**Hand back:** `product_facts`, `offer_and_price`, `limits_and_unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `site_unreachable`: If the product pages will not load, ask the owner for a working URL or the page text, and do not describe the product from memory.
- `price_not_shown`: If no price is visible, record the price as unknown and ask the owner; never guess one.
- `product_behind_login`: If the product can only be seen after signing in, ask the owner for screenshots or a short walk-through; never ask for a password.
- `pages_disagree`: If two pages state different prices or features, list both versions with their URLs and ask the owner which one is current.
- `broken_buy_link`: If a sign-up or buy link fails or lands on the wrong page, report it to the owner before any post sends people there.

### 2. Collect real buyer quotes

Gather the exact words real people use for this problem and the result they want, because copy written in buyers' own words gets read and copy written in the seller's words gets skipped.

- **A-VOC.01** List where real buyers talk about this problem: interviews, reviews, public help threads, customer emails, and refund reasons. Mark which of these the owner allows you to use.  
  _Done when:_ A source list with the URL or origin of each source and whether its use is allowed.
- **A-VOC.02** Collect at least five posts or comments where real people describe this problem in their own words. For each one keep the shortest exact quote, its URL, its date, and an anonymous label such as 'person 1' instead of a name.  
  _Done when:_ At least five quotes, each with URL, date, and anonymous label.
- **A-VOC.03** Sort each quote under one of five headings: the situation they were in, what they do today, what it costs them, the result they want, and what makes them hesitate before buying.  
  _Done when:_ Every quote sits under one heading.
- **A-VOC.04** Remove sellers promoting themselves, AI-written answers, and guesses with no source. If you cannot read the original text, mark it as missing and do not paraphrase it from memory.  
  _Done when:_ A list of what was removed and why.
- **A-VOC.05** Note which kind of people the sample came from (people who have not bought, buyers, people who refunded) and state its limits. Do not present it as the whole market.  
  _Done when:_ A short note on who is in the sample and who is missing.

**Hand back:** `buyer_quotes`, `sources`, `patterns_found`, `limits_and_unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If you find fewer than five real quotes, ask the owner for customer emails or reviews, say which public places you searched, and label everything else as a guess.
- `source_unreadable`: If a page is blocked or needs sign-in, skip it or ask the owner for a copy; do not rebuild the quote from search snippets.
- `private_messages_without_permission`: If the only material is private customer messages, ask the owner whether they may be used and remove names before using them.
- `sensitive_personal_details`: If a quote exposes health, money, or relationship details about an identifiable person, drop the identifying details and keep only the need that relates to the product.

### 3. Group buyers by situation and trigger

Split buyers into groups by the moment the problem shows up, then choose one group to speak to, because copy aimed at one clear situation gets more response than copy aimed at everyone.

- **A-SEGMENTS.01** Read the collected quotes and the product fact sheet. Do not work out why people buy from their age or gender.  
  _Done when:_ Each observation points to a quote or a product fact.
- **A-SEGMENTS.02** Group buyers by five things: when the problem shows up, what they use instead today, what that costs them, the result they want, and the time, money, or skill they have.  
  _Done when:_ Two to four named groups, each described on those five points.
- **A-SEGMENTS.03** Attach at least one real quote to each group, or label the group as a guess. For each group, say who is not a fit.  
  _Done when:_ Each group shows its quote or the word 'guess', plus who it leaves out.
- **A-SEGMENTS.04** Leave ability to pay, who decides, budget, and timing as 'unknown' unless a source says otherwise. Do not answer these from a job title.  
  _Done when:_ A list of unknowns for each group.
- **A-SEGMENTS.05** Choose the one group you can reach on this goal's channel now and that the product can serve today. Record why you chose it and what would show the choice is wrong.  
  _Done when:_ The chosen group, the reason, and at least one case that argues against it.

**Hand back:** `buyer_groups`, `chosen_group`, `why_chosen`, `unknowns`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If there are no buyer quotes, go back and collect them; if the owner wants to continue anyway, label every group as a guess.
- `only_demographics`: If all you have is age, gender, or job title, do not build groups from it; ask the owner about three recent buyers and what happened just before they bought.
- `group_cannot_be_served`: If the best-supported group needs something the product does not do, choose the next group and report the gap to the owner.
- `groups_overlap`: If two groups differ only in name, keep the one with more quotes and drop the other.

### 4. Map each problem to a buying motive

Connect each stated problem to the deeper result the buyer wants, because people pay for a change in their life or work, not for a feature.

- **A-MOTIVES.01** For each group, reread the real quotes and separate the problem they have right now from the change they want in their life or work.  
  _Done when:_ Each group has a 'problem now' line and a 'change wanted' line, both tied to quotes.
- **A-MOTIVES.02** Give each motive a working label such as earning money or career, love or company, health, looks, being liked, saving time, independence, or belonging. Treat every label as a guess to be tested.  
  _Done when:_ A list of motives, each with its label and marked 'to be tested'.
- **A-MOTIVES.03** For each motive write the quote behind it, the situation, how confident you are, and any quote that points the other way. Do not infer sensitive weaknesses from personal data.  
  _Done when:_ Each motive has a quote, a confidence note (strong, some, or guess), and any opposing quote.
- **A-MOTIVES.04** Split each motive into three parts: the practical job they want done, how they want to feel, and how they want others to see them.  
  _Done when:_ Each motive lists its practical, emotional, and social part, with 'unknown' where there is no evidence.
- **A-MOTIVES.05** Keep only the parts the product can really help with. Do not inflate fear, and do not promise income, health, or relationship outcomes.  
  _Done when:_ A final list of motives the product can serve, plus the ones you dropped and why.

**Hand back:** `motives`, `supporting_quotes`, `guesses_to_test`, `limits`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If a motive has no quote behind it, mark it as a guess and go back to collect buyer quotes before relying on it.
- `motive_does_not_fit_a_label`: If a motive does not fit the common labels, describe it in the buyer's own words and do not force it into one.
- `product_cannot_help`: If the product does not really help with a motive, drop it from the selling plan and tell the owner what buyers wanted.
- `sensitive_guess`: If a motive depends on guessing someone's health, finances, or relationship status, remove it and use only needs people stated themselves.

### 5. Pick the strongest motive and objection

Choose the single motive and the single objection that matter most to the chosen group right now, so the post makes one strong point and not five weak ones.

- **A-PRIORITY.01** For the chosen group, read the list of motives, what they use today, and the event that makes them look for a solution. Count repeated quotes from the same person once.  
  _Done when:_ The group name and the quotes used, with repeats from one person merged.
- **A-PRIORITY.02** Compare the motives on four points: how strong the quotes are, how urgent the timing is, what the buyer already pays or loses, and how much of it the product can help with. Judge by this evidence, not by scoring a fixed list of desires.  
  _Done when:_ A short comparison showing each motive against the four points.
- **A-PRIORITY.03** Name the main motive, the second motive, and the cases that contradict them. If the evidence is thin, keep two candidates and say so.  
  _Done when:_ Main and second motive, opposing cases, and a plain statement of how sure you are.
- **A-PRIORITY.04** Separate people who have the budget and the authority to buy from people who only want it. List unknown buying obstacles as questions to ask.  
  _Done when:_ Known objections, plus a list of open questions for the owner or for buyers.
- **A-PRIORITY.05** Pass the main motive on to the benefits step and the angle test. Write down beforehand which real replies or payments would prove it wrong.  
  _Done when:_ The main motive in one sentence and what result would disprove it.

**Hand back:** `main_motive`, `main_objection`, `second_motive`, `what_would_disprove_it`, `open_questions`

**If stuck:**
- `no_real_quotes`: If the ranking rests on no buyer quotes, say it is a guess and go back to collect quotes before treating it as settled.
- `evidence_too_thin`: If the quotes cannot separate two motives, keep both and let the angle test compare them.
- `cannot_tell_who_can_pay`: If nothing shows who has the budget or the say, leave it unknown and ask the owner who has actually paid before.
- `owner_disagrees`: If the owner believes a different motive is strongest, record both views with their evidence and test both.

### 6. Turn features into benefits you can prove

Translate each real feature into the benefit and result a buyer cares about, and mark exactly where proof stops, so the post is persuasive and still true.

- **A-LADDER.01** Read the product facts, the limits, and the buyer quotes one at a time. Do not add anything the product cannot do.  
  _Done when:_ The facts and quotes you used, each traceable to the fact sheet or a source URL.
- **A-LADDER.02** For each feature write a chain of four items: the feature, the benefit a buyer can observe, the result they want, and the point past which nothing may be promised.  
  _Done when:_ One chain per feature with all four items filled in.
- **A-LADDER.03** Mark each link in every chain as verified, guess, or unsupported, and give the reason.  
  _Done when:_ Every link carries one of the three marks and its basis.
- **A-LADDER.04** Check every number and every cause-and-effect claim separately. For example, 'meet people from five industries' and 'raise your chance of a new job' are two claims, and each needs its own proof.  
  _Done when:_ A list of claims, each with its proof or the note 'no proof, do not state as fact'.
- **A-LADDER.05** Choose the value statement that is both true and matches the main motive, and write it as one to three short message options ready to test.  
  _Done when:_ The chosen statement and its message options, each tied to verified facts.

**Hand back:** `chosen_message`, `message_options`, `facts_used`, `must_not_promise`

**If stuck:**
- `no_product_facts`: If the product fact sheet is missing or out of date, go back and read the product before writing any benefit.
- `claim_has_no_proof`: If a benefit has no proof, word it as an invitation to try or as something being tested, never as a result.
- `number_without_source`: If a number has no source, remove it or ask the owner where it comes from.
- `benefit_needs_missing_feature`: If the benefit buyers want depends on a feature that does not exist, drop it from the copy and tell the owner about the gap.

### 7. Design the sales angle test

Set up a fair comparison of two or three ways to present the same true benefit, with the win rule written before posting, so the goal's own number decides which one works.

- **A-ANGLES.01** Name one buyer group in one situation, the quote that represents them, the motive, and the benefit the product can deliver.  
  _Done when:_ Group, quote, motive, and benefit written in four short lines.
- **A-ANGLES.02** Write two or three angles: the progress they hope for, getting past the real obstacle, and a believable demonstration. Do not use fear to exaggerate outcomes.  
  _Done when:_ Two or three angles, each in one or two sentences.
- **A-ANGLES.03** For each angle list the facts that support it, the promises that are still unknown, and the wording that must not be used.  
  _Done when:_ Each angle has supporting facts, unknowns, and banned wording next to it.
- **A-ANGLES.04** Change only one thing between versions and keep the same channel, audience, and destination link. Before posting, write the guess being tested, how long the test runs, the number that counts as a win, and the cost in time or money.  
  _Done when:_ A test plan written before the first post, showing the guess, the duration, the win number, and the cost.
- **A-ANGLES.05** Choose the main number: the goal's metric, shown with this step (for example link clicks divided by views, followers gained, or purchases). Likes are supporting information only, and state when the test stops.  
  _Done when:_ The main number, the rule for stopping, and the versions ready to be written as posts.

**Hand back:** `test_guess`, `angles`, `facts_used`, `win_rule`

**If stuck:**
- `no_main_motive`: If no main motive has been chosen, go back and choose one before writing angles.
- `angle_needs_unproven_claim`: If an angle only works with a claim that has no proof, rewrite it as an invitation to try or drop the angle.
- `too_few_views_expected`: If the account is too small for the versions to get comparable views, say so and report the result as an early signal, not as proof.
- `cannot_measure_main_number`: If the goal's main number cannot be read anywhere, ask the owner how it can be checked, and do not declare a winner from likes.

### 8. Check what search data, analytics, and outlets the owner has

Find out which search reports and site analytics the owner can show, which product pages search engines can index, and which outside places can carry content, so the round measures real numbers and only uses outlets that already exist.

- **C-SE-ACCESS.01** Ask the owner whether the product site is in Google Search Console, and if so for the exact property: a whole domain (example.com) or one URL prefix (https://example.com/). Confirm the property matches the product's own site, not another brand or an old domain.  
  _Done when:_ The property address, its type (domain or URL prefix), and the owner's confirmation that it is the product's site, or a note that there is no Search Console property.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.02** If a property exists, the owner signs in themselves and sends a screenshot or export of the Performance report, or gives read-only access to a person they choose. Never ask for a password. If the site still needs verification or a DNS change, write it down as an owner task and do not change the site yourself.  
  _Done when:_ How the search numbers will reach you (screenshot, export, or read-only access), or the exact verification task left for the owner.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.03** Ask the owner which site analytics they already use, and whether it can show visits by source (organic search, and each outside site that links in) and visits that reached the pricing or sign-up page. Do not install analytics or add tracking code yourself.  
  _Done when:_ The analytics tool's name, whether it splits visits by source, and how the owner will share it (screenshot or read-only access), or a note that there is none.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.04** Fix the settings every later reading will use: country, language, device, search type (web), and time zone. The most recent days of Search Console data are often incomplete, so plan to read only dates at least 3 days old.  
  _Done when:_ One line with country, language, device, search type, time zone, and the 3-day settling delay.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.05** Decide which AI answer tools will be sampled (for example Google's AI answers, ChatGPT search, or Perplexity), in which region and language, and who runs each one: the agent in its own browser if it has one, or the owner by screenshot. Keep AI answer samples separate from Search Console totals; one is never used as the other.  
  _Done when:_ The list of AI answer tools, the region and language, and who will run each one.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.06** Use read_page to open the product's main pages and its /robots.txt. List the pages a buyer would land on, and mark any page that robots.txt blocks or that shows a noindex tag where you can see it. Do not add structured data or new pages; report problems to the owner as product tasks.  
  _Done when:_ A list of product URLs, each marked indexable, blocked, or unknown, with the reason for any block.
- **C-SE-ACCESS.07** List the outside places that can already carry content about the product: the owner's own blog or newsletter, the owner's profiles on publishing sites, and publications that have run the owner's writing before or publicly invite contributions.  
  _Done when:_ A list of outlet URLs, each with how content gets published there and who controls it.

**Hand back:** `search_property`, `report_access`, `indexable_pages`, `outlets`

**If stuck:**
- `no_search_property`: If the site has no Search Console property, continue with public search research only and record search report numbers as not available, never guessed.
- `site_needs_verification`: If adding the property needs a site file or DNS record, submit the step as blocked and tell the owner exactly which record or file Search Console asks for; do not change the site yourself.
- `no_site_analytics`: If the owner has no site analytics that split visits by source, tell them visit-based numbers will rest only on the tracked link's click count, and let them decide whether to add analytics themselves.
- `pages_blocked_from_index`: If key product pages are blocked by robots.txt or noindex, write down the page and the reason and hand it to the owner as a product fix; do not promise any ranking or citation.
- `wrong_property`: If the property the owner shows belongs to another site or brand, do not use its numbers and ask the owner for the property of the product's own site.

### 9. Find the searches buyers type before they buy

Build a list of real buyer searches, grouped by problem, comparison, alternative, and purchase, and checked against live results and the product's facts, so outside articles target searches that bring buyers who can actually use the product.

- **C-SE-QUERY.01** Fix the market for the research before searching: country, language, and the date you search. Use the same settings for every search so results can be compared.  
  _Done when:_ One line with country, language, and search date.
- **C-SE-QUERY.02** Start from the buyer quotes and product facts already collected. Write searches in the buyers' own words, sorted into four groups: problem ('how to ...'), comparison ('X vs Y'), alternative ('X alternative'), and purchase ('X price', 'best X for ...'). Write at least 20 searches with at least 3 in each group, not just category words.  
  _Done when:_ A table of at least 20 searches in four groups, each with the buyer quote or fact it came from.
- **C-SE-QUERY.03** Check each search against the product facts. Remove any search that needs a feature, price, or market the product does not have today, and list it as excluded with the reason.  
  _Done when:_ An excluded list, each search with the missing fact that ruled it out.
- **C-SE-QUERY.04** Run the 10 most promising remaining searches with your own web search. Look at the first 10 results of each and open the top 3. Note what kind of page ranks (guide, comparison, forum thread, product page), whether the product or a competitor appears, and what question the top results leave unanswered.  
  _Done when:_ For each of the 10 searches, the result URLs, the page types, and one unanswered question or 'fully answered'.
- **C-SE-QUERY.05** Where a search shows demand only through forum posts or comments, count how many different people asked it. The same person asking twice, or the same thread seen twice, counts once.  
  _Done when:_ For forum-based searches, the number of different people and the thread URLs.
- **C-SE-QUERY.06** Pick 3 to 5 searches where the product is a true answer and the current results leave a gap. For each, write a short brief for an outside article or guest post: the search, the reader's situation, the gap to fill, the product facts to use, and the product page to link with the round's tracked link.  
  _Done when:_ Three to five article briefs, each tied to one search and one tracked product link.
- **C-SE-QUERY.07** If a search shows the product's own site is missing a page, list it as a site gap for the owner. Do not edit the site.  
  _Done when:_ A list of site gaps, or a note that none were found.

**Hand back:** `query_groups`, `source_urls`, `excluded_queries`, `article_briefs`, `limitations`

**If stuck:**
- `no_buyer_quotes`: If there are no buyer quotes to start from, go back to the buyer quote step before writing searches.
- `search_results_unavailable`: If you cannot run web searches, ask the owner to run the 10 searches and send screenshots of the first page of results.
- `no_volume_data`: If you cannot see how often a search is made, say so in limitations and rank by fit with the product, never by an invented volume.
- `product_fits_no_search`: If almost every search needs features the product lacks, stop and tell the owner which searches buyers make and which facts are missing.
- `no_tracked_link`: If the round has no tracked link yet, call tracking to get one before writing the briefs.

### 10. Confirm the pitch address, routes and story kit for press

Settle which address sends pitches, which kinds of coverage are on the table, and which true facts and stories the brand can offer, so every pitch that follows is credible and costs nothing the owner did not approve.

- **C-PR-ACCESS.01** Ask the owner which email address will send pitches and reply to editors. Prefer an address on the brand's own domain, and check that the domain matches the product site. The owner signs in to that mailbox themselves; never ask for a password.  
  _Done when:_ The sending address, the brand domain, and who will press Send (the owner, or the agent's own email tool if it has one).
- **C-PR-ACCESS.02** Ask the owner whether they already have contacts or past coverage: outlets that featured them, podcasts they joined, newsletters that mentioned them, or a contributor profile on a publication. Open each public page that shows it.  
  _Done when:_ A list of existing relationships and past coverage, each with its URL, or a note that there are none.
- **C-PR-ACCESS.03** Ask the owner which kinds of coverage this round may use: editorial (a writer covers the story for free), guest (guest article, podcast guest, newsletter feature) and paid (sponsorship, paid placement). Paid routes are only listed for the owner, never bought.  
  _Done when:_ The kinds of coverage allowed this round, each labeled editorial, guest or paid.
- **C-PR-ACCESS.04** Build a story kit from things the brand owns: product facts, any original data or research, a short demo, the founder's real background, and images the owner holds the rights to. Ask the owner for a founder bio under 100 words. Never borrow another company's numbers.  
  _Done when:_ The story kit, with the source of every fact and the owner's confirmation that each image may be used.
- **C-PR-ACCESS.05** Ask the owner what must stay private or wait: launch dates, unreleased features, customer names. Use a customer's name or result only if that customer has agreed to it.  
  _Done when:_ A short list of what can be shared now and what cannot, in the owner's words.
- **C-PR-ACCESS.06** Ask the owner for the spending limit in writing. The default is zero. If any outlet quotes a fee for a slot, write down the outlet, the price and what it includes, and bring it to the owner as a decision.  
  _Done when:_ The owner's written spending limit, and any quotes listed as open decisions.
- **C-PR-ACCESS.07** Get the round's tracked link from tracking and open it once to confirm it reaches the product page. This is the link every pitch and every published piece should carry.  
  _Done when:_ The tracked link and confirmation that it loads the product page.

**Hand back:** `sending_address`, `routes`, `story_kit`, `spend_limit`

**If stuck:**
- `no_brand_address`: If the owner has no address on the brand's domain, ask which address they want to use; do not create a new mailbox unless the owner asks for one.
- `nothing_new_to_offer`: If the story kit has no fact, data or story a journalist would find new, tell the owner plainly and suggest what could be built first, instead of pitching with nothing.
- `paid_slot_required`: If a route only works by paying, list the price and terms for the owner and do not buy anything.
- `embargo_or_rights`: If you are unsure whether a fact, name or image may be shared, leave it out until the owner confirms.
- `owner_action_needed`: If signing in asks for a code, a terms screen or an identity check, tell the owner which screen needs them and wait; never enter it for them.

### 11. Find outlets whose audience matches the buyer and invites pitches

Produce a short list of active newsletters, podcasts and publications that reach the buyer and publicly invite pitches or guests, because a pitch only moves the goal's main number when the outlet's readers are the people who buy.

- **C-PR-FIND.01** Write 5 to 10 search phrases from the buyer's situation and problem, such as '<buyer situation> newsletter', '<problem> podcast', '<topic> write for us' or '<topic> be a guest'. Run them with web search and note each phrase and the date.  
  _Done when:_ The list of phrases used, each with the date it was run.
- **C-PR-FIND.02** For each candidate outlet, open its three most recent issues, episodes or articles. Keep it only if it published something in the last 60 days and its recent topics match the buyer's problem. Do not judge an outlet by its subscriber count.  
  _Done when:_ Each outlet's URL, the three recent pieces read, their dates, and a one-line note on who they are for.
- **C-PR-FIND.03** Find how the outlet asks to be contacted: a 'write for us' page, a pitch or guest form, or an editor's address published on the outlet's own site. Record that URL. If there is no public invitation, exclude the outlet. Never guess an address pattern or collect personal emails.  
  _Done when:_ For each kept outlet, the URL of its public contact or submission page.
- **C-PR-FIND.04** Label each route: editorial pitch, guest article, podcast guest, newsletter feature, or paid sponsorship. Put paid sponsorships in a separate list for the owner to decide on.  
  _Done when:_ Each outlet labeled by route, with paid options listed apart.
- **C-PR-FIND.05** For each outlet, write one fit reason that links a recent piece of theirs to a real story, demo or data point from the story kit. Keep what the outlet says it wants separate from your own guess about fit. Drop outlets where the brand has nothing new to add.  
  _Done when:_ A fit reason per outlet, with the outlet's stated wish and your inferred fit written separately.
- **C-PR-FIND.06** Check whether the brand has already pitched each outlet: ask the owner, search the sending mailbox, and, if the owner already uses a CRM, look there too. Exclude any outlet that said no or asked not to be contacted, and any outlet pitched in the last 30 days that has not answered.  
  _Done when:_ Each outlet marked new, already pitched, declined, or waiting, with the source of that status.
- **C-PR-FIND.07** Keep at most 15 outlets, ranked by fit. Hand back three groups: qualified, excluded, and unknown, each with a reason. If fewer than three qualify, report the true number.  
  _Done when:_ The ranked list of up to 15 qualified outlets, plus the excluded and unknown groups with reasons.

**Hand back:** `outlets`, `fit_reasons`, `exclusions`, `search_phrases`

**If stuck:**
- `no_public_contact_route`: If an outlet has no public way to pitch or apply as a guest, exclude it; do not guess an email address.
- `pitch_wrong_desk`: If the only contact covers a different topic or section, look for the right page on the outlet's own site, and exclude the outlet if there is none.
- `already_pitched`: If the brand pitched this outlet before, keep its status and do not add it again this round.

### 13. Send one tailored, approved pitch to each chosen outlet

Send a short, personal pitch to each qualified outlet through the route it invites, and follow it until it goes live or closes, because accepted pieces that carry the tracked link are what bring visitors and inquiries.

- **C-PR-PITCH.01** For each outlet on the list, open its contact or submission page again and confirm it is still open to this kind of pitch. Search the sending mailbox for any earlier pitch, reply, refusal or opt-out. Skip any outlet that declined or asked not to be contacted.  
  _Done when:_ Each outlet marked ready or skipped, with the reason.
- **C-PR-PITCH.02** Write one pitch per outlet. Subject line under 60 characters. Body under 200 words: name a recent piece of theirs, give the angle, two or three facts from the story kit, what you can deliver (an article draft, an interview, data, a demo), and the sender's real name, the brand and their role in it. Follow any word limit or form fields the outlet sets.  
  _Done when:_ One draft per outlet, with its word count and the outlet's own requirements noted.
- **C-PR-PITCH.03** Where the pitch links to the product, use the round's tracked link. If you are testing two angles across similar outlets, add ?v=A to one angle and ?v=B to the other, and change only the angle.  
  _Done when:_ Each draft's link, with its version label if there are two angles.
- **C-PR-PITCH.04** Check every sentence against the story kit. Remove any number, result, customer name or promise you cannot support, and never promise the outlet traffic or sales. Respect anything the owner said must stay private.  
  _Done when:_ Each draft with its claims listed and the source for each.
- **C-PR-PITCH.05** Show the owner each pitch word for word, with the address or form it goes to. Send no more than 10 pitches in a round. Nothing goes out without the owner's yes.  
  _Done when:_ The approved drafts and the owner's approval message.
- **C-PR-PITCH.06** Send each pitch once, to one person or form per outlet. The owner sends it from the sending address, or the agent sends it with its own email tool only if it has one and the owner said yes. For a form, submit it once. If sending seems to fail, check the sent folder or confirmation page before trying again.  
  _Done when:_ For each pitch: recipient or form URL, subject, time sent, and any confirmation number or message link.
- **C-PR-PITCH.07** Wait at least 7 days, or the response time the outlet states, before one short follow-up in the same email thread, approved by the owner. Send no more than one follow-up, and never resend the pitch with a new subject line.  
  _Done when:_ For each outlet: waiting, replied, declined, or followed up once, with dates.
- **C-PR-PITCH.08** When an outlet says yes, share only approved material and bring any fee, contract or paid slot to the owner as a decision. When the piece goes live, open its public URL, note the publish or air date, and check that it carries the tracked link.  
  _Done when:_ For each accepted pitch: what was agreed, the public URL once live, the date, and the link it carries.

**If stuck:**

- `declined_or_opted_out`: If the outlet or person declined or asked not to be contacted, stop and record it; never contact them again this round.
- `pitch_wrong_desk`: If the outlet replies that this is the wrong contact, use only a contact they name or one on their own site; never guess an address.

- `paid_slot_required`: If the outlet asks for payment, give the owner the exact price and terms and do not pay or agree to anything.
- `embargo_or_rights`: If the outlet wants material the owner has not cleared, ask the owner before sending it.
